## 1. Design read

This is **The Croods, but for tech overwhelm.** The visitor scrolls through a
handcrafted claymation world where they are a ragged, lovable caveman — drowning
in stone tablets (unread email), chasing wild boars (deadline chaos), and doing
everything the brutally hard way. In the dark of that jungle, a small, glowing
emerald creature — warm, patient, gently amused, never condescending — finds
them, parts a curtain of vines, and leads them through a threshold of light into
a paradise where things simply **work**: smooth machines, tidy desks, an empty
inbox, a banana-phone that rings politely instead of screaming. There, the
cavemen transform into kempt, glass-wearing, uniformed citizens of a banana and
coconut company — funny, aspirational, and quietly saying the same thing the
whole time: *your business can feel like this too.* The humor is the hook; the
promise is the point.

---

## 2. Concept spine

A six-beat narrative, played entirely through scroll:

1. **Wilderness chaos** — Cavemen do everything the hard way. Overwhelmed, endearing, funny.
2. **The encounter** — A glow in the dark. The Emerald mascot finds them.
3. **The threshold** — A vine curtain parts. The portal. A crossing of light and color.
4. **Enlightened paradise** — An advanced world where everything just works. The first crack of transformation.
5. **The transformed company** — Kempt, uniformed, glassed-up citizens thriving at the banana & coconut company. The comedy pays off.
6. **The welcome** — The world is open. An invitation to the visitor to step in.

**Emotional arc:** *Frazzled → Intrigued → Awed → Relieved → Delighted → Welcomed.*

**The promise (constant):** From tech overwhelm to "it just works." Emerald
Technology Consulting helps time-poor business owners with AI, automation,
notes & systems, and custom AI — this journey is what that feels like.

**The business:** Emerald Technology Consulting (Jordan's consulting practice).
Journey IS the home page at jordanchristley.com; content sections + contact
follow below the journey.

## 3. Journey

Six scenes. All 16:9 landscape, Architecture A continuous forward flight,
~150vh scroll travel each. Scenes 1–2 live in the **Wilderness** grammar,
scenes 4–6 in the **Paradise** grammar, scene 3 is the **Portal** bridge.

### Scene 01 — `wilderness-chaos`

- **Headline:** "Everything takes forever."
- **Subtext:** "This is your day before the glow shows up."
- **Tags:** `wilderness` `overwhelm` `state-a` `comedy-setup`
- **World:** Wilderness
- **Character states:** Brub (State A), Vex (State A)

stub. Beside him, lanky Vex shouts into a hollow gourd (no one answers), while
a piglet evades their attempts to pen it and a fire pit spits grey smoke into

knowing warmth. The whole frame is muddy amber **except** the mascot, which
glows mint and matrix green. Vex points; Brub squints. The glow throws emerald

### Scene 04 — `paradise-first-light`

## 4. Two-world grammar

Two worlds, one camera. Every Wilderness scene prompt MUST open with the
**Wilderness lock**; every Paradise scene prompt MUST open with the **Paradise
lock**. Only the scene subject changes. The portal scene is the seam where one
lock hands off to the other.

### World A — The Wilderness (`[WORLD_A]`)

```
[WORLD] claymation stop-motion, handcrafted felt + clay, visible thumbprints and tool marks
[PERSPECTIVE] low wide angle, eye level to slightly elevated, gentle dolly forward
[PALETTE] raw earth tones — clay mud [#6b4a2f] jungle soil [#4a3426] moss [#5a6b3c]
          dried grass [#8a7f5c] — with ember accents [#e07b39] and charcoal shadow [#2a211c]
[LIGHT] dim smoky firelight, dappled canopy shafts, warm amber falloff, no clean light
[SURFACE] matte clay, rough woven fibers, dust, soot, pitted stone — everything slightly imperfect
[PROPS] stone tablets, coal stubs, gourds, tangled vines, bones, fire pit, untidy piles
[MOOD] frazzled warmth — endearing chaos, never grim. The mess is comedic, not sad.
[NEGATIVE] no text, no logos, no watermarks, no UI elements, no typography, no plastic,
           no electricity, no modern objects, no smooth glass, no clean surfaces
```

**Thematic contrast (World A):** raw earth, warmth that fights for light, rough
surfaces, everything *manual*. The palette is low-saturation mud and moss; light
is scarce and smoky; texture is imperfect and loved.

### World B — The Paradise (`[WORLD_B]`)

```
[WORLD] claymation stop-motion, handcrafted felt + clay, clean and polished — smooth clay,
        glossy lacquer on brass, everything lovingly finished
[PERSPECTIVE] low wide angle, eye level to slightly elevated, gentle dolly forward
[PALETTE] clean emerald + paradise pastels — mint [#d7f5e9] bright emerald [#5cdb8a]
          deep emerald [#2d8a4e] cream [#f7f3e8] — with soft gold accents [#d4a843]
          and a single coral accent [#f2a97f]
[LIGHT] bright even daylight, soft glow from every machine, warm gold rim light — no shadows hiding anything
[SURFACE] smooth polished clay, lacquered brass, softly glowing glass, clean woven cotton
[PROPS] tidy desks, neat tablet stacks, empty inbox, brass buttons, banana-phones,
        coconut name badges, gentle machines, fruit baskets
[MOOD] calm, aspirational, gently wondrous — everything hums, nothing shouts.
[NEGATIVE] no text, no logos, no watermarks, no UI elements, no typography, no dirt,
           no smoke, no tangle, no clutter, no dark corners
```

**Thematic contrast (World B):** clean emerald and cream, light that is easy and
everywhere, smooth and finished surfaces, everything *automatic*. The two worlds
are not "ugly vs pretty" — they are *effort vs ease*, *fumbling vs fluent*. The
palette swap (mud → mint) is the single strongest signal the visitor has crossed.

## 5. Transformation arc

Two named characters transform. Both need **reference elements** (Phase 1.5)
for BOTH states — register them before scene stills are generated so the same
"person" persists through every scene.

### Character manifest (register as reference elements)

| Element | Used in scenes |
|---------|----------------|
| `brub-state-a` — the messy founder caveman | 1, 2, 3 |
| `vex-state-a` — the messy assistant caveman | 1, 2, 3 |
| `brub-state-b` — the kempt founder citizen | 4, 5, 6 |
| `vex-state-b` — the kempt assistant citizen | 5, 6 |
| `emerald-mascot` — the guide (single form, both worlds) | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 |

### State A — The Messy Caveman (Wilderness)

- **Brub** (founder): stocky, broad brow, matted dark hair pointing every
  direction, dirt smudge on one cheek, torn patched fur pelt, knotted vine belt
  with a stone tablet tucked in it, permanently furrowed brow, smudged hands.
- **Vex** (assistant): lanky, skinny, big front teeth, hair like a startled
  bush, clutches a coconut in one arm at all times, fur pelt tied on crooked,
  one missing sandal.
- **Posture:** hunched, tired, squinting at everything, arms full, always mid-task.
- **Hygiene:** caked dirt, soot on forearms, greasy hair.

### Midpoint beat (Scene 04 — `paradise-first-light`)

**Brub gets his glasses first.** Round, gold-rimmed, a touch too big. His hair
has been combed back and one patch of dirt is scrubbed clean — the first kempt
detail. He stands up straighter. **Vex is still State A** — coconut, ragged fur,
startled hair — gaping in wonder. The two cavemen are deliberately at different
stages in the same frame: *the change is happening, one piece at a time.* This
single beat is the whole narrative in miniature.

### State B — The Kempt Citizen (Paradise)

- **Brub** (founder): same body, transformed — tidy dark hair, clean skin,
  pressed beige caveman uniform with clean white collar and **emerald piping**,
  gold-rimmed glasses, small **coconut-shaped name badge** reading BRUB. Posture
  relaxed, upright, confident; holds a banana-phone with ease.
- **Vex** (assistant): kempt bright hair, clean pressed uniform matching Brub,
  tidy coconut badge reading VEX, glasses, and — new detail — a neat pair of
  sandals that match. Posture springy, cheerful, hands free.
- **Posture:** open, calm, proud; everything they do is smooth and easy.
- **Hygiene:** immaculate. The dirt is gone; the *person* is unchanged.

**Transformation is cosmetic, not identity.** The cavemen become better-fed,
better-kempt versions of themselves — not different people. That is the promise
to the visitor: *you don't have to become someone else; you just have to stop
doing everything the hard way.*

## 6. The Emerald Mascot

A single small claymation character — **one form, both worlds** — the connective
tissue of the whole journey.

- **Visual identity:** a single large emerald gemstone cut like a **brilliant-cut
  diamond — but entirely green**. Deep dark royal-forest-green facets (shadowed,
  near-black green spots) contrast with **bright glowing matrix-like green light**
  shining out from inside the gem's cut edges and core. It floats upright, softly
  bobbing, radiating light. Hand-rolled clay, a few fingerprints left visible — it
  is clearly *made*, not rendered. In the dark Wilderness it is the only bright
  light in the frame; in Paradise it blends in but keeps its inner glow.
- **Role:** the guide — the "Betterman" of The Croods, but soft. It never speaks
  (a gem doesn't need dialogue); it *demonstrates*. It parts the vine curtain, it
  lights the way, it flicks the light switch, it watches proudly. It is patient and
  never condescending — it finds the cavemen mid-chaos and simply shows them the
  door.
- **Scenes:** 2 (first appearance), 3 (leading through the portal), 4 (guiding),
  5 (watching proudly from the shelf), 6 (at the threshold, beckoning the visitor).
- **Does it transform?** **No.** It is the constant — the same in both worlds,
  the thing that is *already* home in Paradise and never out of place. Its
  stability is the anchor the visitor recognizes across the scroll.
- **Identity lock:** **LOCKED** — reference element `emerald-mascot`
  (`3970ee59-a7ae-4d11-8f83-f97a0ea862ac`). Visual: brilliant-cut diamond-shaped
  emerald, deep forest-green facets, matrix glow. Source:
  `docs/assets/emerald-mascot.png`. Its inner matrix glow must persist in every
  scene, Wilderness or Paradise.

## 7. Portal / threshold crossing

The world-crossing seam (Scene 03). Creative direction:

- **The vine curtain:** the mascot parts a woven curtain of jungle vines like a
  theatre curtain — a literal, handcrafted threshold. The vines' textures are
  the last thing of the Wilderness we see up close.
- **The glow tunnel:** parting the vines reveals a short tunnel of soft light —
  not harsh, not white-out; a warm mint-and-gold tunnel where **bananas and
  coconuts fall in slow, weightless fruit-rain**, each one glowing as it passes
  the camera. Claymation physics: fruit floats like it's under water, drifting,
  never rushing.
- **The color shift:** this is the money moment. The same frame shifts from the
  Wilderness lock to the Paradise lock — mud and amber bleeding into mint and
  cream, matte becoming glossy, smoke becoming clean light. Done as a continuous
  forward push so it reads as *passing through*, not cutting.
- **The handoff:** the **last Wilderness frame and the first Paradise frame are
  the same image** — only the palette and finish differ. Architecture A's exact
  frame handoff makes this seamless: extract the last frame of leg 3, and the
  first frame of leg 4 is that same frame re-lit.
- **Aftermath detail (payoff, Scene 06):** the fruit-rain appears once more in
  the open doorway of the welcome scene — but now it lands gently in neat
  baskets. The chaos, tamed.

## 8. Comedic beats

Warm, whimsical, gentle satire of tech overwhelm. The jokes land because the
caveman *is* the visitor. Setup in Wilderness, payoff in Paradise.

| Beat | Setup (Wilderness, scenes 1–2) | Payoff (Paradise, scenes 4–6) |
|------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **The light switch** | Brub grunts and fails to light the fire with rocks, blowing on embers, smoke in his face. | Brub flicks a single brass light switch and the room fills with warm light — his face is pure wonder. |
| **The inbox** | A wobbling tower of stone tablets he must chip at one-by-one with coal; more keep sliding in. | A tidy desk, a neat stack, and an **empty inbox** — he pats it fondly. |
| **The banana-phone** | Vex shouts into a hollow gourd; no reply; he shakes it, confused. | A small glowing banana-phone rings *politely, once*; Brub answers, nods, sets it down. Bliss. |
| **The machine** | Cavemen chase a runaway piglet that will not be penned; all of work is wrangling. | Vex taps one brass button; a machine dispenses exactly one coconut. No fuss. |
| **The exhibit** | The old way is everywhere and exhausting. | The stone tablets are mounted on the office wall in a neat row — *"how it used to be,"* a museum of their former selves. |
| **The missing sandal** | Vex is missing one sandal in State A. | State B Vex has a neat matching pair — a running gag that pays off in one close-up frame. |

**Tone guardrails:** never mock the visitor. The humor is always *with* the
caveman, never *at* him — the joke is on the *old process*, not the person stuck
in it. Comedic, never cruel; gentle satire of tech overwhelm, never a dunk.

## 9. Camera architecture

- **Architecture A — Continuous Forward Flight** (vid-scroll-scrub default).
  One camera, one journey, no cuts. **Scrolling IS the camera.**
- **Motion:** a slow, steady forward drift — the pace of walking through a
  story, never a race. Consistent velocity across all six legs so seams feel
  like one continuous shot. Ease in at the journey start, ease out at the end.
- **Seam direction:** every leg ends with the camera moving forward at the same
  speed it begins the next leg — **last frame of leg N = first frame of leg N+1**
  (extract with `ffmpeg -sseof -1`, feed as `--start-image` / `start_image`).
  No speed jumps at boundaries; velocity must match on both sides of every seam.
- **Scroll travel:** ~150vh per scene so scroll delta maps cleanly to video
  duration at midpoints (vid-scroll-scrub failure-mode #13).
- **Reverse scroll:** full support — scroll up plays the journey backward.
  This is a feature; the forward flight works in both directions.
- **Vertical placement:** keep the camera gently at character height (low wide,
  slightly elevated) so the cavemen and mascot feel reachable, not filmed from above.
- **Reduced motion:** `prefers-reduced-motion` falls back to static posters with
  semantic chapter labels (scene names), never an empty hole.

## 13. Delivery budget

- **Per scene (desktop):** ≤ 10 MiB encoded clip
- **Per scene (mobile):** ≤ 5 MiB encoded clip
- **Codec:** H.264 / yuv420p
- **Desktop encode:** CRF 20, GOP 8, `-movflags +faststart`, no audio, unsharp
- **Mobile encode:** CRF 23, GOP 4, 720p cap, `-movflags +faststart`, no audio, unsharp
- **Audio:** **none** — scroll-scrub sites need no audio (and this journey is
  silent-filmmaking by design; the comedy reads visually)
- **Poster:** exact first frame of the **encoded** clip (never the source still),
  so poster matches what the browser paints at frame 0
- **Six scenes, six desktop legs + six mobile legs + twelve posters**
